CRISC Exam-Day One-Pager

Think like a risk practitioner: advise, analyze, report. The owner decides.
150 questions4 hoursScaled 200 to 800, pass 4504 domains D1 Governance 26%D2 Risk Assessment 22%D3 Risk Response and Reporting 32%D4 Technology and Security 20%

The practitioner mindset filters

  1. Advise, and the owner decides. You analyze, recommend, and report. The accountable risk owner selects the response and accepts residual risk.
  2. Business objectives first. Risk only matters in relation to what the business is trying to achieve. Start with objectives and context.
  3. Assess before you respond. Identify and analyze (or reassess after a change) before choosing controls, tools, or escalation.
  4. Process and owner before tools. A named owner and a working process come before any technology purchase.

Ownership rules

  • Board and executive management: approve risk appetite; ultimately accountable for risk governance.
  • Risk owner (business): accountable for the risk; selects the response; accepts residual risk and exceptions.
  • Control owner: accountable for a specific control’s design and day-to-day operation, and keeps its evidence.
  • Risk practitioner (second line): facilitates, analyzes, monitors, reports. Never accepts risk for the owner.
  • Data owner (business): classifies data, approves access. Custodian (IT): implements protection.
  • Internal audit (third line): independent assurance. Does not own controls or make management decisions.

15 trap answers, and what to choose instead

1Fix it yourself. Advise and coordinate; the owner acts.
2Practitioner accepts the risk. Only the risk owner accepts residual risk.
3Buy a tool to close the gap. Start from objectives, owner, and process.
4Implement controls right away. Assess the risk first, then respond.
5Pick the most secure option. Pick the cost-justified option within appetite.
6KRI breached: raise the threshold. Escalate on the defined path to the owner.
7Insurance transfers the risk fully. It shares cost; accountability stays.
8Rank vulnerabilities by CVSS alone. Use exploitation evidence + asset criticality.
9Send the board the full register. Top risks vs appetite, trends, decisions needed.
10Late action plan: reset or close it. Report the delay and exposure to the owner.
11Interview the control owner as proof. Inquiry is weakest; reperform a sample.
12IT owns and classifies the data. The business data owner does.
13Vendor SOC 2 exception: terminate. Evaluate the impact and follow up on remediation.
14Open-ended or verbal exception. Documented, time-bound, compensating, owner-accepted.
15Block the new technology (AI). Find out how it is used, assess, then govern.
✓Tie-breaker: if two answers look right, choose the one that informs the owner with analysis tied to objectives.

Metric types

KRIRisk exposure; leading; threshold tied to appetite. Unpatched critical servers.
KCIControl performance. % privileged accounts reviewed on time.
KPIGoal or process performance. % projects delivered on schedule.

Response options

AcceptWithin appetite: risk owner signs off. Above appetite: needs higher approval. Keep monitoring.
MitigateControls reduce likelihood or impact.
Transfer / shareInsurance, contracts. Accountability stays.
AvoidStop the activity that creates the risk.

Control functions

Preventivestops the event (MFA, SoD)
Detectivefinds it (log review, KRI alert)
Correctivefixes it (restore, patch)
Deterrentdiscourages it (warning banner)
Compensatingsubstitutes when the primary is not feasible

Categories: administrative, technical, physical.

Design effectiveness: could it work? Operating effectiveness: did it work over the period? Test a sample across time.
